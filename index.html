WeatherTop

Bring the weather indoors. Weather on your desktop.

Getting started

WeatherTop lives in your menu bar as a small cloud. Click the cloud to start or stop the weather. Right-click it (or Control-click) to choose rain, snow, hail, fog, leaves and more, and to change intensity, wind, sound and other options.

Common questions

I can't find the cloud in my menu bar

On a MacBook with a camera notch, a full menu bar hides icons that don't fit. Open WeatherTop again from your Applications folder to see the welcome window, and press ⌃⌥⌘W to start or stop the weather. To make room for the cloud, hold ⌘ and drag other menu bar icons out of the way.

The weather disappears when I watch a video or use a full-screen app

That's on purpose: WeatherTop steps aside for full-screen apps, videos and games and comes back when you leave them. To keep it showing, turn on Options ▸ Show Over Full-Screen Apps.

How do I turn on sound?

Right-click the cloud and choose Sound Effects, then set the volume with the slider that appears.

Can it run like a screen saver?

Yes. Choose Start When Idle and a delay. The weather drifts in when you step away and fades the moment you touch the mouse, keyboard or trackpad. It waits while a video, presentation or call is keeping your display awake.

Can it start when I log in?

Use Startup ▸ Open at Login. By default the weather stays off until you click the cloud; choose Start the Weather to have it begin right away, and pick which weather it opens with.

Does it slow my Mac down or use a lot of battery?

WeatherTop draws with the GPU and uses very little processor time. In Low Power Mode it halves its frame rate, and it pauses while your displays sleep or your screen is locked.

Will the weather show up when I share my screen?

It can. Turn on Options ▸ Hide from Screen Sharing to ask macOS to leave it out; some apps may still capture it.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit WeatherTop from its menu, then drag it from Applications to the Trash.
